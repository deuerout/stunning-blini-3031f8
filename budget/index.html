Emergency fund

Where £950 stretches further

UK schemes worth checking

Council Tax discounts

Living alone gets 25% off automatically if you claim it. On a low income, apply to your council's Council Tax Reduction scheme too.

Often £30–£60 a month

Help to Save

If you get Universal Credit, save up to £50 a month and the government adds a 50% bonus after 2 and 4 years. Put your emergency fund here.

Up to £1,200 in bonuses

Broadband social tariffs

Most big providers offer £12–£20 a month packages if you receive Universal Credit or similar benefits. Ask to switch mid-contract without a fee.

£10–£20 a month

SIM-only phone plan

Once your handset is paid off, a SIM-only deal on a budget network costs £5–£10 a month for plenty of data.

£10–£25 a month

Check every entitlement

Run a free benefits calculator — many people on £950 a month miss help they qualify for. Try Turn2us or entitledto.

Can be £100s a month

Food without the waste

Shop Aldi or Lidl with a list, plan 3 batch-cook meals a week, and check Too Good To Go or Olio for surplus food near you.

£30–£50 a month

Energy help

Ask your supplier about the Warm Home Discount and fuel-debt support schemes; submit meter readings monthly so you're never overbilled.

£150 one-off (if eligible)

Travel cheaper

Weekly or monthly bus tickets beat daily fares. A Railcard pays for itself after one or two longer trips — 1/3 off rail fares.

£10–£30 a month

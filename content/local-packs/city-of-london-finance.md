## Google Business Profile update (London listing)

86% of UK financial services firms now use AI. Only 2% trust their AI agents to act with broad autonomy.

That gap is where City firms are stuck. The FCA's October 2026 review made it plain: there will be no new senior manager for AI. Accountability sits under the existing SM&CR and Consumer Duty, and governance bolted on after a tool goes live is now named as poor practice.

Deverout and Associates works with City of London banks, insurers and asset managers to move AI from pilot to permanent: redesigning decision chains so that every point where a model acts has a named owner and a clear customer outcome, and building the skills inside your teams to run it.

Structural, not cosmetic.

## LinkedIn post

The City has adopted AI. It has not yet rebuilt around it.

86% of UK financial services firms now use AI.
80% have deployed AI agents.
2% let those agents act with broad autonomy.

That last number is the story.

The tools arrived. The operating model didn't move. AI sits beside the old process as a pilot, while the process, and the accountability around it, stays as it was.

In October the FCA closed the escape route. No dedicated Senior Manager Function for AI. Accountability stays where it already is, under SM&CR and the Consumer Duty. And governance retrofitted after deployment is now named, in writing, as poor practice.

So the question for City boards is not "how do we scale the pilot?"
It is "which process should exist at all once this capability is native, and who answers for it?"

A policy document cannot answer that. A redesigned decision chain can.

What is your firm's 2%?

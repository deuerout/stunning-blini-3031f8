What buyers actually require

The two routes into large Jamaican supply chains work differently. Government buys through formal, documented tenders. Hotels buy on ordering cycles, quality specifications and trust built over time.

Government procurement

Rules-based and documented. Missing paperwork is the most common reason bids fail.

  1. Watch GOJEP every weekThe Government of Jamaica Electronic Procurement portal lists tenders from ministries, agencies and public bodies. Set alerts for your category.
  2. Get your documents currentBusiness registration with the Companies Office of Jamaica, TRN, a valid Tax Compliance Certificate from TAJ, and NIS and other statutory compliance.
  3. Register as a supplier where requiredMany contracts require registration with the Public Procurement Commission in the right category and grade. Check the tender’s eligibility section.
  4. Read the whole tender documentNote the specification, evaluation criteria, mandatory forms, bid validity period, and any bid security or declarations.
  5. Ask clarifications in writing, before the deadlineProcuring entities publish answers to all bidders. Questions after the cut-off are not answered.
  6. Price completely and submit on timeInclude GCT treatment, delivery and every listed line. Late or incomplete bids are rejected, regardless of price.
  7. Perform, document, and build a recordDelivery notes, signed receipts and on-time invoices get you paid and become references for the next bid.

Tourism & hotel supply

Relationship-led and specification-driven. Consistency matters more than a low first price.

  1. Find the purchasing manager, not the front deskLarge properties have a purchasing or stores department; smaller ones buy through the executive chef or general manager.
  2. Learn their ordering cyclePerishables are ordered daily or several times a week, dry goods and amenities monthly, furniture and equipment on capital budgets. Fit your offer to the cycle.
  3. Meet the quality specificationSize, grade, ripeness, packaging and labelling are specified. Food suppliers should expect food-handler permits, BSJ labelling and traceability questions.
  4. Complete vendor approvalExpect a vendor form, TRN and TCC, insurance, and for food a site visit or sample approval. Group-owned hotels may need head-office approval.
  5. Plan for the seasonVolumes peak in the winter season (mid-December to April) and drop in late summer and autumn. Agree minimum and maximum weekly volumes.
  6. Hit the receiving windowDeliveries are checked at the receiving dock at set times against the purchase order. Short, late or off-spec deliveries are refused.
  7. Invoice cleanly and wait for termsPayment commonly follows 30 to 60 days after a correct invoice. Plan working capital before you sign (see tool 4).

The City has adopted AI. It has not yet rebuilt around it.

Transformation for banks, insurers, asset managers and market infrastructure in the Square Mile and Canary Wharf: moving AI from pilot to permanent, under governance that a senior manager can put their name to.

01 The Local Challenge

The Bank of England and FCA's 2026 survey found that 86% of UK financial services firms now use AI, up from 75% in 2024. Eight in ten respondents had deployed AI agents. Only 2% let those agents act with broad autonomy.

That gap is the City's real story. The tools have arrived; the operating model has not moved. AI sits beside the old process as an assistant, a pilot, a proof of concept, while the process itself, and the accountability around it, stays exactly as it was.

The regulator has noticed. In October 2026 the FCA set out good and poor practice on AI and named the failure plainly: governance retrofitted after a tool was already deployed, and gaps between where risk is identified and where a senior manager is accountable for it. There will be no dedicated Senior Manager Function for AI. Responsibility sits inside the Senior Managers and Certification Regime and the Consumer Duty as they already stand. Delegating a decision to a model does not delegate the liability.

02 Three Pressures

Structural, not cosmetic.

Accountability without architecture. Boards are being asked to show reasonable steps over systems whose behaviour is hard to predict. A policy document cannot answer that. A redesigned decision chain, with named owners at each point a model acts, can.

Pilots that never become the business. When agents are deployed but kept on a short leash, the firm pays for two operating models and gets the benefit of neither. The question is not how to scale the pilot. It is which process should exist at all once the capability is native.

A workforce asked to change faster than it is taught. The Financial Services Skills Commission measured a 35 percentage-point gap between demand for AI-related skills and the talent available. Almost every role will change; very few people need to become experts. Most firms are hiring for the 1.5% and neglecting the rest.

03 How We Work in the City

We ask whether the system should exist, then build the one that should.

Our practices, applied to the pressures above.

AI transformation

From pilot to permanent. We map each AI use to the decision it changes, the customer outcome it touches under the Consumer Duty, and the senior manager who answers for it, then redesign the process around that chain rather than bolting governance on afterwards.

Digital transformation

Resilience as architecture. Firms now run important business services through a small number of cloud and model providers. We trace those dependencies through your operational resilience mapping and design for the outage that will come.

Strategic repositioning

When AI changes what a broker, an underwriter or a relationship manager is for, it changes what the firm is for. We read the long arc of the market and reposition before the margin moves.

Capability that compounds

Liberation, not extraction. Every engagement leaves your people able to run the new model without us, closing the skills gap from inside rather than renting it.
